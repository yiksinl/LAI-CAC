What is leaf area index?

Leaf area index, or LAI, describes how much leaf surface a landscape contains relative to its ground area.

An LAI of 3 means roughly 3 square meters of leaves for every square meter of ground. The number can exceed 1 because leaves overlap in layers.

Higher LAI means more leaf area—not necessarily healthier plants.

    Methods and limitations

    Readiness and validation

    • Checking installed assets and validation status…

    These checks establish preprocessing compatibility, not prediction accuracy.

    LeafView applies Yixuan Li's selected 600-tree XGBoost model to GOES-19 red, near-infrared, and shortwave-infrared reflectance statistics, viewing and illumination geometry, seasonality, and IGBP land cover. The model preserves separate 15, 18, and 21 UTC composites.

    Each window contains eight UTC calendar dates. Seasonality is calculated from the window's start date with the notebook's formula. LeafView only uses windows whose observations are all after the model's April 6, 2026 training cutoff. Results after that cutoff test how the trained model transfers to later observations; they are not forecasts.

    Rolling-window construction and numerical consistency are checked separately. Prediction accuracy for rolling eight-day windows has not been evaluated.

    The training reference is a quality-screened MODIS/VIIRS satellite consensus, not direct field ground truth. LAI is leaf area per ground area; it is not a direct plant-health score, drought diagnosis, or irrigation recommendation.
