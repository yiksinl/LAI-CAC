What is leaf area index (LAI)?

LAI 3 means 3 m² of leaves per m² of ground. Leaves overlap in layers. More leaf area doesn’t necessarily mean healthier plants.

LAI reference

Published landscape averages
1.3Deserts
1.7Grasslands
3.6Crops
4.8Tropical rainforests
5.1Temperate deciduous forests
5.5Temperate conifer forests

Values vary with season and location.

Sources

These biome averages come from the global field-observation synthesis by Asner and colleagues after statistical outliers were removed. They are reference examples—not category boundaries, health ratings, or limits for a LeafView result.

Leaf area also changes with vegetation type, season, rainfall, growth stage, canopy structure, and measurement method. A LeafView satellite area may contain a mixture of land covers.

Supporting references: Asner, Scurlock, and Hicke (2003), global LAI synthesis; Sibanda and colleagues (2019), seasonal savanna measurements; Parker and Tibbs (2004), Maryland deciduous forest phenology.

      How LeafView works

      How LeafView estimates leaf area

      LeafView uses a machine-learning model developed by Yixuan Li during his CISESS research. It estimates leaf area from GOES-19 satellite observations, using reflected light, viewing and illumination angles, season, and land cover.

      The model was trained against quality-screened MODIS and VIIRS satellite LAI estimates. These are satellite-based reference values, rather than direct field measurements.

      Observation periods and history

      Each estimate combines observations from eight consecutive UTC dates. The latest estimate uses a rolling window that advances daily. It represents the entire eight-day period, rather than conditions on one day.

      The history chart shows monthly snapshots, each based on an eight-day observation window—not a monthly average. Exact dates are available for each point, and periods without sufficient observations remain gaps.

      LeafView uses only observations after April 6, 2026, the model’s training cutoff. It estimates leaf area from observations collected after training; it does not forecast future conditions.

      Processing checks and validation

      Processing checks have confirmed compatibility with the supplied model and preprocessing, rolling-window construction, and numerical consistency.

      These checks establish implementation consistency, not prediction accuracy. Accuracy for rolling eight-day estimates has not yet been evaluated.

      The original training-period results remain unreproduced because the historical feature rows are unavailable.

      Interpreting the results

      LAI describes leaf area per unit of ground area across the sampled satellite footprint. That footprint may contain a mixture of trees, fields, roads, buildings, and other land cover.

      The number of usable observations indicates how much data contributed to an estimate. It is not a confidence or accuracy score.

      Results can help users explore estimated leaf area and changes over time. LAI alone cannot diagnose plant health or drought, explain the cause of a change, or determine watering needs.

      Technical details

      LeafView uses the selected 600-tree XGBoost model with the original feature definitions and preprocessing. It preserves separate composites for observations around 15, 18, and 21 UTC. Seasonality is calculated from each window’s start date using the original notebook formula.

      About this project

      Created by Yixuan Li, inspired by his summer research internship at CISESS. LeafView makes his leaf-area model accessible through an interactive map.
